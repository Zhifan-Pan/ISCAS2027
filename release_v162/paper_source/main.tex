\begin{abstract}
Fallback decoding can repair recognition errors, but its value depends on both candidate acquisition and acceptance. We isolate these decisions using a text-compression trigger and fixed four-feature selector. On 7,500 frozen Common Voice recordings, selective recovery reduces macro word error rate by 2.26 percentage points and matches unconditional recovery in paired error counts. On development-exposed TEST, Direct achieves lower mean error with multi-temperature fallback, whereas reselection is better with single-pass sources. Matched-bank comparisons reveal that expected-risk fitting's advantage over pairwise ranking depends on edit-gap weighting. Population-weighted timing estimates for reranking systems show savings up to 5.15\% over unconditional recovery on a fixed stratified sample; Native remains faster. Code is available at \url{https://github.com/Zhifan-Pan/ISCAS2027}.
\end{abstract}

multilingual speech recognition, selective recovery, candidate acquisition, N-best reranking, confidence estimation, conditional computation

\section{Introduction}
An acquisition rule that preserves useful recognition corrections under one selector may discard them under another. We study this dependence by separating candidate acquisition from acceptance.

Multilingual N-best reranking~\cite{gaur2022,yan2025wild}, discriminative rescoring~\cite{rescorebert,discriminative2023,audioattention2019}, voting~\cite{fiscus1997} and confusion networks~\cite{mangu2000} exploit existing hypotheses. Generative correction extends candidate banks~\cite{kuo2026framework,dutta2022,progres2024,whisperingllama}; risk-based pruning and ASR sampling control candidate computation~\cite{cheng2023pruning,jinnai2025mbrasr}.

Conditional computation allocates effort through recognizer cascades~\cite{xue2025sima}, selective multi-pass recognition~\cite{aleksic2025}, encoder proxies~\cite{fathullah2024proxies}, selective correction~\cite{lim2025hybrid} or language-specific distillation~\cite{ferraz2024distil}. Confidence need not predict additional computation's benefit~\cite{jitkrittum2023cascade}: acceptance determines which acquired hypotheses improve the output.

We freeze the recognizer and known language labels, controlling acquisition within each source. A frozen panel evaluates the original policy; retrospective comparisons test acceptance objectives~\cite{mwer2018,ranknet2005,goel2000mbr} and acquisition budgets.

\section{Controlled Acquisition and Acceptance}
\subsection{Recording-level acquisition}
Per window $i$, we normalize and deduplicate five beams into $B_i$, retaining first occurrences and sources; $b_i$ is the raw top beam. Fallback $g_i$ expands this to $F_i=B_i\cup\{g_i\}$, with at most six candidates. The outer ratio $\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text}}$ divides top-beam UTF-8 byte length by its zlib-compressed length, with zero for empty bytes. Define $z_i=\mathbf1\{\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text},i}>2.4\}$ and $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=\max_i z_i$. Any trigger acquires fallback for every window, including singleton banks.

Recovery uses the final Hugging Face fallback output, with temperatures $0,0.2,\ldots,1$, compression threshold $1.35$, log-probability threshold $-1$ and no-speech threshold $0.6$. Internal compression uses serialized token-ID bytes, differing in scale from the outer text ratio. Transformers v4.57.6 documents $1.35$ as a common value~\cite{hfwhisper4576}. Native HF denotes this matched configuration. Retries are conditional; intermediate hypotheses never expand the bank.

\subsection{Four-feature Shared acceptance}
The selector uses $f(y)=(a,p,t,r)$ for candidate $y$. Decoder score $a$ averages teacher-forced body-token log probabilities. Length $t=\log(1+T)$ uses the same $T$ tokens, excluding prefix, padding and end tokens. XLM-R-base pseudo-log-likelihood $p$~\cite{xlmr,mlmscoring}, abbreviated PLL, scores the original string by masking each nonspecial subword individually in a centered window of at most 510 subwords and averaging the target log probabilities. This differs from within-word masking~\cite{kauf2023}. Zero body tokens give $a=t=0$, and zero PLL targets give $p=0$. Text that becomes empty only after normalization need not have zero features.

For normalized words $w_{1:M}$, define the overlapping $n$-gram count $c_y(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{w_{k:k+n-1}=u\}$. Repetition is
\begin{equation}
r_{n,h}(y)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{c_y(w_{j:j+n-1})\ge h\}}{\max(1,M-n+1)}.
\label{eq:repetition}
\end{equation}
We use $r=r_{4,3}$, which is zero for $M<4$. Repetition is also a target of unlikelihood training~\cite{welleck2020}.

The centered vector $x_i(y)=f(y)-f(b_i)$ preserves ranking and softmax probabilities. The vector $s\in\mathbb R^4_{>0}$ gives feature-wise RMS scales over training-fold pairs $\{(i,k):i\in\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L\mathcal I_\ell,\ y_{ik}\in C_i\setminus\{b_i\}\}$, where $C_i$ is the fitting bank; scales below $10^{-12}$ become one. Define edit benefit as $\Delta_{ik}=E(b_i,y_i^*)-E(y_{ik},y_i^*)$ and probabilities as $\pi_{ik}=\operatorname{softmax}_k\!\left(\theta^\top\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}x_{ik}\right)$. Expected-risk fitting~\cite{mwer2018} minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1L\sum_{\ell=1}^L\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_\ell}\sum_k\pi_{ik}\Delta_{ik}}{N_\ell}+\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2.
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I_\ell=\{i:\operatorname{lang}(i)=\ell\}$ indexes fitting windows, $N_\ell$ counts their reference words and $L=5$. Greedy-expanded DEV yields a language-shared $\beta_F=\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}\theta$ per run. Shared retains this selector across sources, with bank-order ties. Define $\hat{y}_i^B=\arg\max_{y\in B_i}\beta_F^\top x_i(y)$, $C_i(0)=B_i$ and $C_i(1)=F_i$. Then
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{y}_i^{S+R}&=\arg\max_{y\in C_i(z^{\mathrm{rec}})}\beta_F^\top x_i(y),\\
\hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{Direct}}&=\begin{cases}\hat{y}_i^B,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=0,\\g_i,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=1.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}\label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
B reranks the original bank, whereas N+R requests fallback for every window and reranks the expanded bank. Direct bypasses selection and PLL on triggered recordings, retaining B elsewhere. On single-window CV27, $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=z_i$; multi-window FLEURS uses the recording flag in both branches. Selected window transcripts are concatenated before complete-reference scoring. Shared thus isolates acquisition from acceptance changes.

\subsection{Matched acceptance controls}
G4 denotes the original $\beta_F$. H4 refits expected risk on complete HF-expanded DEV banks, and H3 refits the same objective using only $(a,t,r)$. HR4 uses RankNet~\cite{ranknet2005} on H4's banks and four features. Its logistic loss favors the lower-edit candidate in every unequal-edit pair, with language-reference normalization and equal total weight per bank. HWR4 additionally weights each pair by $|E_j-E_k|/c$, where $c$ is the training-fold mean edit gap under the original pair weights.

Refits use five folds formed by within-language connections through clients and reference texts. RMS uses each training fold, and held-fold hard macro WER selects among 49 log-spaced penalties from $10^{-4}$ to $1$, with stronger regularization on ties. Full-DEV fitting uses zero-initialized L-BFGS-B. Singletons retain their errors and reference denominators without contributing ranking pairs.

Minimum Bayes risk selection~\cite{goel2000mbr} minimizes expected word edit count within the same bank. U-MBR uses uniform mass, while D-MBR uses $\operatorname{softmax}(Ta/\tau)$ over body scores rather than calibrated sequence posteriors. DEV WER selects $\tau\in\{0.1,0.3,1,3,10\}$, preferring larger values on ties. Candidate ties retain bank order.

A \emph{complete policy} uses its own acceptor on both banks. A \emph{triggered-subset evaluation} changes acceptance only on triggers, retaining G4 outputs elsewhere and full-panel reference denominators. H3Direct uses H3 off-trigger and the final HF candidate on-trigger.

\section{Data and Evaluation}
Using 44.60\,h of FLEURS TRAIN~\cite{fleurs}, we adapt Whisper-small~\cite{whisper} to Arabic, Persian, Polish, Turkish and Italian with LoRA~\cite{lora} across three different random seeds: 4,656 updates each, rank and scale four, zero dropout, all attention projections. CV27 TRAIN is excluded from recognizer adaptation; FLEURS DEV is excluded from reported fits. Selectors and gates use CV27 DEV. Complete TEST contains 3,665 FLEURS and 58,514 Common Voice 27.0 recordings~\cite{cv2020}, release \texttt{2026-09-11}. TEST informed development, making these comparisons retrospective.

Confirmation uses 7,500 CV27 TRAIN recordings, 1,500 per language, excluding previously accessed recordings, audio and reference texts. Predictions, stopping rules and the 0.10\,pp one-sided noninferiority margin were locked before reference evaluation under procedural controls. Its 10,000 synchronized within-language speaker-component bootstrap draws account for within-panel correlation. Recurring speakers across Common Voice splits restrict generalization to the observed speaker-clustered sampling frame. Later gates, refits and window policies remain retrospective.

Decoding uses known languages, bfloat16, beam size five, length penalty one, native suppression and a 444-token cap in Transformers 4.57.6. At 16\,kHz, $N$ samples give $J=\lceil N/480000\rceil$ windows bounded by $\lfloor jN/J\rfloor$, without previous-window text conditioning. Deduplication, WER and $r$ share NFC, digit unification, punctuation-to-space, Persian mapping and Turkish casing~\cite{normalization}. Source RNG mappings remain fixed.

WER aggregates within languages before averaging five languages and three seeds; calls pool seed--recording instances. Seed diagnostics follow a consistent order.

TEST intervals use 2,000 paired draws of language-internal client/reference components, synchronized across seeds and conditional on frozen fits and source draws. FLEURS controls are descriptive because component metadata are unverified.

Costs sample 255 FLEURS and 472 CV27 recordings: a first-checkpoint census of triggers or Native-output changes plus ten ordinary recordings per language--duration-quintile cell. Inclusion probabilities permit population weighting, but five FLEURS and 207 CV27 later-checkpoint triggers are unsampled; their latency impact is unknown. Both studies use three randomized, paired A40 rounds with both models resident. Each path reuses its encoder and first search without cross-path sharing; PLL batches 32 masks. Synchronized waveform-to-output timing excludes loading, warmup and interfered blocks. Intervals use 4,000 paired stratified bootstrap draws with finite-population correction and equal, synchronized checkpoint weights in A, conditional on measured sessions.

\section{Conclusion}
Sparse acquisition preserves Shared's observed recovery gain while reducing unconditional-recovery latency. Direct and matched-bank controls show that source and acceptance objective shape accuracy, with benefits varying across languages and seeds.

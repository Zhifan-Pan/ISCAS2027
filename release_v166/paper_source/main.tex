\begin{abstract}
Fallback recovery depends on both candidate acquisition and acceptance. We separate these decisions using a text-compression trigger and fixed four-feature selector. On 7,500 frozen Common Voice recordings, selective recovery lowers macro word error rate by 2.26 percentage points and matches unconditional recovery's error counts in every seed. Retrospective CV27 TEST comparisons retain 99.98\% of positive edit gain. On matched banks, unweighted ranking trails expected-risk training across seeds, whereas advantages from direct adoption and edit-gap weighting depend on source and checkpoint. Timing across three checkpoints estimates up to 5.15\% savings over unconditional reranked recovery, although Native remains faster. Code is available at \url{https://github.com/Zhifan-Pan/ISCAS2027}.
\end{abstract}

multilingual speech recognition, selective recovery, candidate acquisition, N-best reranking, confidence estimation, conditional computation

\section{Introduction}
Additional decoding improves recognition only when useful candidates are accepted. We separate acquisition from acceptance to study this interaction in multilingual ASR.

Multilingual N-best reranking~\cite{gaur2022,yan2025wild}, discriminative rescoring~\cite{rescorebert,discriminative2023,audioattention2019}, voting~\cite{fiscus1997} and confusion networks~\cite{mangu2000} exploit existing hypotheses. Generative correction expands banks~\cite{kuo2026framework,dutta2022,progres2024,whisperingllama}, while risk-based pruning and ASR sampling allocate computation~\cite{cheng2023pruning,jinnai2025mbrasr}. Conditional computation uses recognizer cascades~\cite{xue2025sima}, selective passes~\cite{aleksic2025}, encoder proxies~\cite{fathullah2024proxies}, selective correction~\cite{lim2025hybrid} or language-specific distillation~\cite{ferraz2024distil}. Confidence need not predict computational benefit~\cite{jitkrittum2023cascade}, because acceptance determines which acquired hypotheses improve output.

With recognizer and language labels fixed, we compare acquisition within sources. A frozen panel evaluates the original policy, while retrospective controls assess acceptance objectives~\cite{mwer2018,ranknet2005,goel2000mbr} and acquisition budgets.

\section{Controlled Acquisition and Acceptance}
\subsection{Recording-level acquisition}
Normalizing and deduplicating five beams per window $i$ forms $B_i$, retaining first occurrences and sources. With raw top beam $b_i$ and fallback $g_i$, the expanded bank $F_i=B_i\cup\{g_i\}$ contains at most six candidates. The outer $\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text}}$ divides top-beam UTF-8 byte length by zlib-compressed length, assigning zero to empty bytes. With $z_i=\mathbf1\{\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text},i}>2.4\}$ and $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=\max_i z_i$, any trigger acquires fallback for every window, including singletons.

Recovery follows Hugging Face temperatures $0,0.2,\ldots,1$ with compression, log-probability and no-speech thresholds $1.35$, $-1$ and $0.6$. Internal compression uses serialized token-ID bytes, unlike the outer text scale. Transformers v4.57.6 lists $1.35$ as common~\cite{hfwhisper4576}. Native HF uses the same configuration, with conditional retries and only the final output entering the bank.

\subsection{Four-feature Shared acceptance}
For features $f(y)=(a,p,t,r)$, decoder score $a$ averages teacher-forced log probabilities over $T$ body tokens, excluding prefix, padding and end tokens, while $t=\log(1+T)$. XLM-R-base pseudo-log-likelihood $p$~\cite{xlmr,mlmscoring}, or PLL, averages individually masked nonspecial subword log probabilities in centered contexts of at most 510 subwords of the original string, rather than within-word masking~\cite{kauf2023}. Missing body tokens give $a=t=0$ and missing PLL targets give $p=0$, whereas normalization alone need not produce zero features.

For normalized words $w_{1:M}$, the sequence-wide overlapping count is $c_y(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{w_{k:k+n-1}=u\}$. We measure repetition by
\begin{equation}
r_{n,h}(y)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{c_y(w_{j:j+n-1})\ge h\}}{\max(1,M-n+1)}.
\label{eq:repetition}
\end{equation}
We use $r=r_{4,3}$, which is zero for $M<4$. Repetitive sequences also motivate unlikelihood training~\cite{welleck2020}.

Centering $x_i(y)=f(y)-f(b_i)$ preserves rankings and softmax probabilities. Feature-wise RMS scales $s\in\mathbb R^4_{>0}$ use training-fold pairs $\{(i,k):i\in\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L\mathcal I_\ell,\ y_{ik}\in C_i\setminus\{b_i\}\}$ in fitting banks $C_i$, replacing scales below $10^{-12}$ with one. With edit benefit $\Delta_{ik}=E(b_i,y_i^*)-E(y_{ik},y_i^*)$ and $\pi_{ik}=\operatorname{softmax}_k\!\left(\theta^\top\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}x_{ik}\right)$, expected-risk fitting~\cite{mwer2018} minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1L\sum_{\ell=1}^L\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_\ell}\sum_k\pi_{ik}\Delta_{ik}}{N_\ell}+\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2.
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I_\ell=\{i:\operatorname{lang}(i)=\ell\}$ indexes fitting windows, $N_\ell$ counts reference words and $L=5$.

Shared weights $\beta_F=\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}\theta$ are fitted per run on greedy-expanded DEV and fixed across sources, resolving ties by bank order. With $\hat{y}_i^B=\arg\max_{y\in B_i}\beta_F^\top x_i(y)$, $C_i(0)=B_i$ and $C_i(1)=F_i$, the policies are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{y}_i^{S+R}&=\arg\max_{y\in C_i(z^{\mathrm{rec}})}\beta_F^\top x_i(y),\\
\hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{Direct}}&=\begin{cases}\hat{y}_i^B,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=0,\\g_i,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=1.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}\label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
B reranks $B_i$, whereas N+R requests and reranks $F_i$ unconditionally. Direct bypasses selection and PLL on triggers, retaining B elsewhere. CV27 has $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=z_i$, while multi-window FLEURS uses the recording flag. Window transcripts are concatenated for full-reference scoring, with Shared fixing acceptance to isolate acquisition.

\subsection{Matched acceptance controls}
G4 uses the original $\beta_F$. H4 refits expected risk on complete HF-expanded DEV banks, while H3 uses $(a,t,r)$. HR4 fits RankNet~\cite{ranknet2005} on H4's banks and four features, favoring lower-edit candidates in unequal-edit pairs with language-reference normalization and equal total bank weight. HWR4 weights pairs by $|E_j-E_k|/c$, where $c$ is the training-fold mean edit gap under the original pair weights.

Five folds group connected clients and reference texts within languages. Each training fold determines RMS scales, and held-fold macro WER selects among 49 log-spaced penalties from $10^{-4}$ to $1$, favoring stronger regularization on ties. Full-DEV fitting uses zero-initialized L-BFGS-B. Singletons retain errors and reference denominators without contributing ranking pairs.

Minimum Bayes risk decoding~\cite{goel2000mbr} minimizes expected word edits on the same banks using uniform mass in U-MBR or body scores $\operatorname{softmax}(Ta/\tau)$ in D-MBR. These scores are not calibrated sequence posteriors. DEV WER selects $\tau\in\{0.1,0.3,1,3,10\}$, preferring larger values on ties, while candidate ties retain bank order. A \emph{complete policy} applies its own acceptor to both banks. A \emph{triggered-subset evaluation} changes only triggered acceptance, preserving G4 elsewhere and full-panel denominators. H3Direct uses H3 elsewhere and final HF on triggers.

\section{Data and Evaluation}
We adapt Whisper-small~\cite{whisper} on 44.60\,h of FLEURS TRAIN~\cite{fleurs} to Arabic, Persian, Polish, Turkish and Italian using LoRA~\cite{lora} with three different random seeds. Each run uses 4,656 updates, rank and scale four, zero dropout and all attention projections. Adaptation excludes CV27 TRAIN, while selectors and gates use CV27 DEV rather than FLEURS DEV. Complete TEST contains 3,665 FLEURS and 58,514 Common Voice 27.0 recordings~\cite{cv2020} from release \texttt{2026-09-11}. Its use during development makes TEST comparisons retrospective.

Confirmation uses 7,500 CV27 TRAIN recordings, 1,500 per language, excluding previously accessed recordings, audio and reference texts. Predictions, stopping rules and the one-sided 0.10 percentage-point (pp) margin were locked before reference evaluation under procedural controls. Within-panel correlation is addressed by 10,000 synchronized bootstrap draws of speaker components within languages. Recurring speakers across splits restrict generalization to the observed speaker-clustered sampling frame, while later gates, refits and window policies remain retrospective.

Decoding uses known languages, bfloat16, five beams, length penalty one, native suppression and a 444-token cap in Transformers 4.57.6. At 16\,kHz, $N$ samples give $J=\lceil N/480000\rceil$ windows bounded by $\lfloor jN/J\rfloor$ without previous-window text conditioning. Deduplication, WER and $r$ share NFC normalization, digit unification, punctuation-to-space, Persian mapping and Turkish casing~\cite{normalization}, with fixed source RNG mappings.

WER aggregates within languages before averaging five languages and three seeds, whereas calls pool seed--recordings. TEST intervals use 2,000 paired draws of client/reference components within languages, synchronized across seeds and conditional on frozen fits and source draws. FLEURS comparisons are descriptive because component metadata are unverified.

Costs use 255 FLEURS and 472 CV27 recordings, covering first-checkpoint triggers or Native-output changes plus ten ordinary recordings per language and duration quintile, weighted by inverse inclusion probability. Five FLEURS and 207 CV27 later-checkpoint triggers are unsampled, or 0.05\% and 0.12\% of seed--recordings in strata with nonzero inclusion probabilities. Triggered N+R and S+R perform identical acquisition and scoring (zero paired algorithmic difference). Study A's relative savings (5.15\% and 4.57\%) closely match Study B's single-checkpoint complete census (5.18\% and 4.54\%). Both studies use three randomized paired A40 rounds, resident models, encoder/first-search reuse and 32-mask PLL batches. Waveform-to-output timing excludes loading, warmup and interfered blocks. Session-conditional intervals use 4,000 paired stratified bootstrap draws with finite-population correction and synchronized, equally weighted checkpoints in A.

\section{Conclusion}
Sparse acquisition matches unconditional recovery across three frozen-panel seeds and reduces latency within reranked pipelines. Expected-risk fitting consistently beats unweighted ranking, whereas Direct and edit-gap weighting depend on source and checkpoint. Separating these decisions supports selective acquisition under a fixed scorer while showing why acceptance and fallback generation require joint evaluation.

\begin{abstract}
We separate fallback acquisition from acceptance using a compression trigger and a fixed four-feature selector. On a locked confirmation panel of 7,500 Common Voice 27.0 (CV27) recordings, selective recovery lowers macro word error rate by 2.26 percentage points (pp) over baseline rescoring and matches unconditional recovery's error counts in every seed. Retrospective CV27 TEST comparisons show 99.98\% retention of macro-weighted positive edit gain. Reselection improves unscreened fallbacks; direct adoption can outperform the fixed selector on internally screened HF outputs. Matching acceptance to HF banks with edit-gap weighting yields the lowest exploratory CV27 WER of 31.44\%. Across three checkpoints, selective acquisition saves up to 5.15\% latency within reranked pipelines. Native HF remains faster but incurs 1.46\,pp higher WER on FLEURS and over 2.1\,pp on CV27. Code is available at \url{https://github.com/Zhifan-Pan/ISCAS2027}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Multilingual ASR encounters both routine recognition errors and catastrophic repetition loops. Additional decoding helps only when its output improves on existing candidates and the scoring rule accepts it. Separating acquisition from acceptance reveals when extra decoding is useful and when it wastes computation.

Multilingual N-best reranking~\cite{gaur2022,yan2025wild}, discriminative rescoring~\cite{rescorebert,discriminative2023,audioattention2019}, voting~\cite{fiscus1997} and confusion networks~\cite{mangu2000} exploit existing hypotheses. Generative correction expands banks~\cite{kuo2026framework,dutta2022,progres2024,whisperingllama}, and risk-based pruning and ASR sampling allocate computation~\cite{cheng2023pruning,jinnai2025mbrasr}. Conditional computation uses recognizer cascades~\cite{xue2025sima}, selective passes~\cite{aleksic2025}, encoder proxies~\cite{fathullah2024proxies}, selective correction~\cite{lim2025hybrid} or language-specific distillation~\cite{ferraz2024distil}, but confidence need not predict the benefit of deferral~\cite{jitkrittum2023cascade}.

We distinguish routine beam rescoring from sparse recovery of repetition loops. The locked confirmation panel isolates acquisition under a fixed selector. Retrospective comparisons examine how fallback screening affects reselection versus direct adoption. On matched banks, we compare expected risk, pairwise ranking and minimum Bayes risk~\cite{mwer2018,ranknet2005,goel2000mbr}. The resulting accuracy--cost tradeoff applies to reranked pipelines; Native HF offers faster decoding at higher WER.

\section{Controlled Acquisition and Acceptance}
\subsection{Recording-level acquisition}
We normalize and deduplicate five beams per window $i$ into $B_i$, with first occurrences and sources retained. With raw top beam $b_i$ and fallback $g_i$, $F_i=B_i\cup\{g_i\}$ contains at most six candidates. Before recovery, $\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text}}$ is the ratio of top-beam UTF-8 byte length to zlib-compressed length, with zero for empty bytes. The fixed gate $z_i=\mathbf1\{\mathrm{CR}_{\mathrm{text},i}>2.4\}$ gives $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=\max_i z_i$. Any trigger expands every window, even singletons (Fig.~\ref{fig:workflow}), to preserve recording-level invocation. On single-window CV27 audio, $z^{\mathrm{rec}}=z_i$; Section~IV-D examines local routing for multi-window FLEURS.

Recovery follows Hugging Face temperatures $0,0.2,\ldots,1$ with compression, log-probability and no-speech thresholds $1.35$, $-1$ and $0.6$. The internal compression check uses serialized token-ID bytes~\cite{hfwhisper4576}, whereas the outer gate uses decoded text to decide whether to request recovery. Native HF uses the same configuration with conditional retries and returns the final transcript without N-best rescoring.

\subsection{Four-feature shared acceptance}
For features $f(y)=(a,p,t,r)$, $a$ is the mean teacher-forced log probability over $T$ body tokens without prefix, padding or end tokens, and $t=\log(1+T)$. XLM-R-base pseudo-log-likelihood $p$~\cite{xlmr,mlmscoring}, or PLL, averages individually masked nonspecial subword log probabilities in centered contexts of at most 510 subwords of the original string, rather than within-word masking~\cite{kauf2023}. Empty outputs have no body tokens or PLL targets and map to $f(\emptyset)=(0,0,0,0)$, but normalization alone need not yield zero features.

For normalized words $w_{1:M}$, the sequence-wide overlapping count is $c_y(u)=\sum_{k=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{w_{k:k+n-1}=u\}$. We measure repetition by
\begin{equation}
r_{n,h}(y)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{M-n+1}\mathbf1\{c_y(w_{j:j+n-1})\ge h\}}{\max(1,M-n+1)}.
\label{eq:repetition}
\end{equation}
We use $r(y)=r_{4,3}(y)$, which is zero for $M<4$. Repetitive sequences also motivate unlikelihood training~\cite{welleck2020}.

Centering $x_i(y)=f(y)-f(b_i)$ preserves rankings and softmax probabilities.
The empty output thus provides an implicit abstention baseline with score $-\beta_F^\top f(b_i)$. We compute feature-wise root-mean-square (RMS) scales $s\in\mathbb R^4_{>0}$ over training-fold pairs $\{(i,k):i\in\bigcup_{\ell=1}^L\mathcal I_\ell,\ y_{ik}\in C_i\setminus\{b_i\}\}$ in fitting banks $C_i$; scales below $10^{-12}$ are set to one. Define edit benefit $\Delta_{ik}=E(b_i,y_i^*)-E(y_{ik},y_i^*)$ and $\pi_{ik}=\operatorname{softmax}_k\!\left(\theta^\top\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}x_{ik}\right)$. The softmax and loss cover all $k\in\{1,\ldots,|C_i|\}$, including $b_i$ with $x_i(b_i)=\mathbf0$ and $\Delta_{i,b_i}=0$. Expected-risk fitting~\cite{mwer2018} minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L(\theta)=-\frac1L\sum_{\ell=1}^L\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_\ell}\sum_{k=1}^{|C_i|}\pi_{ik}\Delta_{ik}}{N_\ell}+\lambda\|\theta\|_2^2.
\label{eq:risk}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal I_\ell=\{i:\operatorname{lang}(i)=\ell\}$ indexes fitting windows, $N_\ell$ counts reference words and $L=5$.

The shared weights $\beta_F=\operatorname{diag}(s)^{-1}\theta$ are fitted per run on greedy-expanded DEV and fixed across sources, with ties broken by bank order. With $\hat{y}_i^B=\arg\max_{y\in B_i}\beta_F^\top x_i(y)$, $C_i(0)=B_i$ and $C_i(1)=F_i$, the policies are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{y}_i^{S+R}&=\arg\max_{y\in C_i(z^{\mathrm{rec}})}\beta_F^\top x_i(y),\\
\hat{y}_i^{\mathrm{Direct}}&=\begin{cases}\hat{y}_i^B,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=0,\\g_i,&z^{\mathrm{rec}}=1.\end{cases}
\end{aligned}\label{eq:accept}
\end{equation}
Policy B reranks $B_i$, and N+R requests and reranks $F_i$ unconditionally. S+R and Direct share acquisition and use B off-trigger, but Direct bypasses selection and PLL on triggers. Window transcripts are concatenated for full-reference scoring.

\subsection{Matched acceptance controls}
G4 keeps $\beta_F$. On complete HF-expanded DEV banks, H4 refits expected risk over four features and H3 omits PLL with $(a,t,r)$. On the same banks and four features, HR4 fits RankNet~\cite{ranknet2005} on unequal-edit pairs with language-reference normalization and equal total bank weight. HWR4 weights pairs by $|E_j-E_k|/c$, where $c$ is the training-fold mean edit gap under the original pair weights.

We group connected clients and reference texts into five folds within each language. Each training fold determines RMS scales, and held-fold macro WER selects among 49 log-spaced penalties from $10^{-4}$ to $1$, with stronger regularization on ties.
After selection, L-BFGS-B refits on full DEV from zero. Singletons contribute errors and denominators but no ranking pairs, and padding is excluded from fitting and selection.

Minimum Bayes risk decoding~\cite{goel2000mbr} minimizes expected word edits using uniform mass in U-MBR or uncalibrated body scores $\operatorname{softmax}(Ta/\tau)$ in D-MBR. DEV WER selects $\tau\in\{0.1,0.3,1,3,10\}$ with preference for larger values on ties; candidate ties retain bank order. A \emph{complete policy} uses its acceptor on both banks, whereas \emph{triggered-subset evaluation} retains G4 off-trigger and full-panel denominators.

\section{Data and Evaluation}
We adapt Whisper-small~\cite{whisper} on 44.60~h of FLEURS TRAIN~\cite{fleurs} across Arabic, Persian, Polish, Turkish and Italian using LoRA~\cite{lora} over three random seeds (4,656 updates, rank and scale four, zero dropout across all attention projections). LoRA training excludes CV27 TRAIN, and selectors and gates are tuned on CV27 DEV. Evaluation on FLEURS TEST (13.07~h; 3,665 recordings) and CV27 TEST (83.00~h; 58,514 recordings)~\cite{cv2020} is retrospective because both splits were accessed during development.

The locked confirmation panel contains 7,500 CV27 TRAIN recordings, 1,500 per language, with no previously accessed recordings, audio or reference texts. Predictions, stopping rules and the one-sided 0.10\,pp noninferiority margin were locked before reference evaluation under procedural controls. We account for within-panel correlation with 10,000 synchronized bootstrap draws of speaker components within languages. Recurring speakers across splits restrict generalization to this speaker-clustered sampling frame. Confirmatory evidence covers the original G4 acquisition policy; later gates, refits and window policies remain retrospective.

Decoding uses known languages, bfloat16, five beams, length penalty one, native suppression and a 444-token cap in Transformers 4.57.6. At 16\,kHz, $N$ samples give $J=\lceil N/480000\rceil$ windows bounded by $\lfloor jN/J\rfloor$ without previous-window text conditioning. Deduplication, WER and $r$ share NFC normalization, digit unification, punctuation-to-space, Persian mapping and Turkish casing~\cite{normalization}, with fixed source RNG mappings. WER aggregates within languages before averaging five languages and three seeds, and calls pool seed--recordings. TEST intervals use 2,000 paired draws of client/reference components within languages, synchronized across seeds and conditional on frozen fits and source draws. FLEURS comparisons are descriptive because component metadata are unverified.

We profile 255 FLEURS and 472 CV27 recordings with inverse inclusion-probability weights. We include all first-checkpoint triggers or Native HF output changes and ten ordinary recordings per language and duration quintile. Study A measures four paths on three checkpoints; Study B measures seven on one. Five FLEURS and 207 CV27 later-checkpoint triggers are unsampled despite nonzero inclusion probabilities and account for 0.05\% and 0.12\% of seed--recordings. Triggered N+R and S+R acquire and score identical banks. Study A's savings of 5.15\% and 4.57\% are close to Study B's 5.18\% and 4.54\% under complete trigger coverage for its checkpoint. Both use three randomized paired A40 rounds with resident models, within-path encoder and first-search reuse, and 32-mask PLL batches. Timing from waveform to output excludes loading, warmup and interfered blocks. Peak CUDA memory in MiB is the maximum whole-call allocation across recordings and rounds with resident models. Session-conditional intervals use 4,000 paired stratified draws and finite-population correction. Study A uses equal checkpoint weights and synchronized draws across paired rounds.

\section{Conclusion}
Selective acquisition matches unconditional recovery across seeds on the locked confirmation panel and reduces overhead within reranked pipelines. On matched HF banks, retrospective controls favor expected-risk fitting over unweighted ranking in every seed. Gains from direct adoption and edit-gap weighting depend on the fallback source and checkpoint.
